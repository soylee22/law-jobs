01How these were judged

Four things decided the order, in this sequence.

  1. Do you clear the stated bar? The PQE line is what actually rejects people. A role asking five years or more is not a stretch, it is a wasted evening. Up to about three years is arguable on four years of Chinese IP litigation. Two years or under you clear outright.
  2. Is the work yours? Trade marks, designs, copyright, brand protection, enforcement, contentious work, cross-border. Not patent prosecution: you are a solicitor, not a CITMA or CIPA attorney, so patent attorney, formalities and renewals roles are out no matter how well they appear to match.
  3. Does the China side help or box you in? It helps at Chinese companies building out Europe, and at any brand enforcing into Asia. It boxes you in where the role is really a China desk.
  4. How crowded is it? Applicant numbers are shown where the advert gave them. A perfect role with two hundred applicants is worth less than a good role with twenty-five and a bar you clear.
Ignore the automatic match scores. They read job titles, not requirements. One role scored 90 and wants six to ten years' experience. Another was titled "Junior Legal Counsel", scored 62, and wants five years plus native Hungarian in Budapest. Three of the four roles that scored 100 are paralegal jobs. Everything below is from reading the actual advert.

04The one thing to fix

After reading thirty-two adverts, the pattern is obvious and it is not about subject matter. The years-of-experience line is what stops you, every time.

The market is full of trade mark and brand roles. Almost all of them want four years or more. You have four years of Chinese practice that most recruiters will not count, and a shorter English record that they will. So every single application has to do the same job: turn the Beijing years into something a recruiter reads as experience rather than as background.

That is one problem, not twelve. It is worth writing that answer once, properly, and reusing it in every covering letter and every recruiter call.

On timing. Sage and Cursitor were both posted on 24 July with under twenty-five applicants each. Both will be crowded inside a week. Of the strong matches found since 20 June, every single one closed before an application went in. These roles last two to three weeks, not two to three months.
