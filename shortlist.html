01What changed since the first version

The first list leaned on job titles with "IP" in them. That was wrong, and it mixed up two different jobs.

You are an IP solicitor, not a trade mark attorney. Trade mark attorney and patent attorney roles need CITMA or CIPA qualification, and the day job is filing and prosecution. Those are excluded now, however well they scored. So are paralegal, formalities and renewals grades. What counts is an IP or brands seat at a law firm, or an IP or brand counsel job in-house, where the work is advisory and contentious.

Your strength is soft and contentious. Trade marks, passing off, copyright, designs, brand enforcement, disputes. Patent work is fine as part of a broader IP disputes practice, and your electrical engineering degree and the US ITC case make it credible, but a role that is only patent prosecution is the wrong job. General commercial and contracts work is a legitimate second lane.

The search was also widened well past the automated scan, which only covers a set list of employers and is heavily weighted toward patent attorney firms. Added this time: the specialist IP job boards, The Lawyer and TotallyLegal, the CITMA board, and the careers pages of fourteen brands and IP firms.

02How the scoring works

Every role gets a score out of 100 from four things. No employer bonuses, no keyword tricks.

WhatRange
Lane. Contentious soft IP 40 · non-contentious soft IP 34 · IP litigation including patent 30 · IP-adjacent, meaning media rights, copyright, brand and advertising 24 · general commercial 140–40
Band. You clear the stated bar 30 · under a year short 22 · one to two years short 12 · more than two years short, or attorney-qualified only 00–30
Employer. How much the name and the seat are worth to the next move, not how famous it is0–15
Odds. Applicant count and how fresh the advert is. Under 30 applicants scores 15, over 150 scores 20–15
Why the old numbers were wrong. The automatic score reads titles and employers and never opens the advert. It gave 90 to a role wanting six to ten years, 62 to a "Junior" role wanting five years and native Hungarian, and 100 to three paralegal jobs. Every number below comes from a human reading the actual requirements.

04Two channels worth more than any single advert

Register properly with five recruiters

They control the elite firm IP seats, and none of those adverts name the firm.

Between them these five are currently carrying an IP and technology associate role at an elite US firm (2–4 PQE, with the advert saying candidates outside that range will also be considered), a commercial IP and IT seat at another US firm (2+ PQE), an IP seat at a Tier 1 TMT firm paying up to £140,000 covering patents, trade marks, designs and copyright (3+ PQE), and a commercial technology role at a leading UK firm covering copyright and trade marks (2–4 PQE). G2 Legal also advertised a 1–3 PQE trade mark associate seat that has already gone, which is proof this band exists and that you have to hear about it early.

The five: G2 Legal, EJ Legal, Gardner Halliday, Noble Legal and SSQ. A twenty-minute call with each, once, is worth more than another evening of applications, because they see the roles before the boards do and they can explain your four Beijing years to a partner in a way a form cannot.

Write speculatively to the brands boutiques

The firms that do exactly your work do not advertise.

Fourteen IP and brands firms' careers pages were checked. The specialist brand firms, the ones whose entire practice is trade marks and brand disputes, have no live junior vacancies and mostly do not use job boards at all. Brandsmiths says "check back soon" and invites a CV and covering letter direct to its recruitment address. Waterfront invites speculative applications from qualified associates and refuses agency candidates. Briffa says it is always looking for talent in IP whether you are at the start of your career or further on.

This is the channel for your niche, and it is not a fallback. A short, specific letter to Stobbs, Briffa, Waterfront, Bristows, Bird & Bird, Rouse, Harbottle & Lewis, Sheridans and Simkins costs one evening in total and reaches firms that will never appear on any of the boards above. Rouse is worth naming twice: a global IP firm with a very large China practice, and dual PRC and English qualification is rare there in London.

One to chase, not to send: you applied to Brandsmiths for an NQ litigation role on 14 June and have had no reply. That chase was due at the end of June.

06What sixty adverts actually show

There are six real IP roles in the entire visible UK market at your level, and you can only comfortably clear the bar on two of them. That is the finding. It is not a reason for gloom, it is a reason to change tactics.

The years-of-experience line is what stops you, every time. Almost every trade mark and brands seat wants four years or more. You have four years of Chinese practice that most recruiters will not count, and a shorter English record that they will. Every application has to solve that same problem, so write the answer once, properly, and reuse it in every letter and every recruiter call.

Because the advertised IP market is this thin, the two channels in section 05 matter more than the adverts. The specialist brand firms hire through speculative letters and through five recruiters, not through job boards. That is where the roles you want actually are.
